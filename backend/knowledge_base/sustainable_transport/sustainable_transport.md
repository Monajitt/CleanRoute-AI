# Principles of Sustainable Urban Transport

## The Urban Mobility Hierarchy
Sustainable urban transport prioritizes transit modes according to environmental efficiency, public health benefits, and spatial footprint:
1. **Active Mobility (Walking, Wheelchairs, Bicycles):** Zero tailpipe emissions, lowest physical space requirement, highest positive health co-benefits.
2. **Public Transit (Electric Rail, Metros, Low-Emission Buses):** High passenger-per-vehicle capacity, low carbon emissions per passenger-kilometer, reduced traffic gridlock.
3. **Shared Mobility & Micro-Mobility (E-Bikes, E-Scooters):** Efficient first- and last-mile connectivity bridging transit hubs and residential zones.
4. **Private Motor Vehicles:** Highest carbon intensity, spatial inefficiency, significant contributor to urban particulate and noise pollution.

## Global Sustainability Goals (UN SDG 11)
The United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 11.2 calls for providing access to safe, affordable, accessible, and sustainable transport systems for all by 2030, notably by expanding public transport and non-motorized active travel infrastructure.

## Environmental Co-Benefits
Shifting personal commuter journeys from internal combustion vehicles to active or clean-air routes directly mitigates local nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$) and fine particulate matter ($PM_{2.5}$), while contributing to regional greenhouse gas emission reductions.

## CleanRoute AI System Mission
CleanRoute AI bridges the gap between atmospheric science and daily commuting decisions by enabling users to factor ambient environmental quality directly into multimodal journey planning. All recommendations are informational tools designed to empower conscious urban travel decisions.
