# Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2) in Urban Corridors

## Chemical Identity and Combustion Origin
Nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$) is a pungent, reddish-brown toxic gas belonging to the nitrogen oxides family ($NO_x$). It forms primarily when atmospheric nitrogen reacts with oxygen at the high temperatures found inside internal combustion engines, particularly diesel engines and heavy-duty commercial transports.

## Roadside Gradients and the Urban Canyon Effect
$NO_2$ concentrations are strongly localized around traffic arteries:
- **Exhaust Proximity:** The highest concentrations occur within 10 to 30 meters of roadway centerlines, diminishing rapidly as one moves into adjacent residential neighborhoods.
- **Urban Street Canyons:** Multi-story buildings flanking narrow roadways trap vehicular exhaust, preventing natural atmospheric mixing and causing $NO_2$ concentrations to build up throughout morning and evening rush hours.
- **Diurnal Fluctuation:** $NO_2$ peaks sharply during commuter peak hours and decreases during midday as solar irradiance triggers photochemical transformations.

## Significance for Sustainable Travelers
For pedestrians and cyclists, traveling directly inside an urban street canyon during peak traffic results in substantially higher $NO_2$ inhalation than traveling along an alternative corridor set back just one or two blocks from the main arterial thoroughfare.
