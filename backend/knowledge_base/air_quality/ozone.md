# Ground-Level Ozone (O3)

## Formation and Atmospheric Chemistry
Ground-level ozone ($O_3$) is distinct from the beneficial stratospheric ozone layer that shields the planet from ultraviolet radiation. At ground level, ozone is a secondary pollutant created by photochemical reactions between nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) and volatile organic compounds ($VOCs$) in the presence of intense sunlight and elevated ambient temperatures.

## Temporal and Geographic Patterns
Ground-level ozone exhibits distinct spatial and temporal behaviors compared to primary combustion emissions:
- **Afternoon Peaks:** Ozone concentrations typically build steadily throughout the morning, reaching peak levels in the early-to-late afternoon when solar irradiance is strongest.
- **Suburban Dispersion:** Because newly emitted nitric oxide ($NO$) from vehicle exhausts temporarily scavenges and titrates ozone in dense urban centers, highest ozone concentrations often occur downwind in suburban or peripheral green corridors.
- **Seasonal Sensitivity:** Ozone levels are markedly higher during late spring and summer months under stagnant high-pressure meteorological systems.

## Relevance to Active Commuters
Because ozone formation is driven by sunlight and warmth, outdoor afternoon commuters—such as recreational cyclists and runners—are particularly vulnerable to ozone-related airway irritation and reduced lung capacity during high-ozone days.

## Application in CleanRoute AI
CleanRoute AI monitors near-current ozone levels from meteorological and environmental feeds. When afternoon ozone concentrations rise, the recommendation engine factors this secondary pollutant into the route's overall estimated exposure, balancing route duration against ambient concentration.
