# Inhalable Coarse Particulate Matter (PM10)

## Overview of PM10
PM10 refers to inhalable airborne particles with aerodynamic diameters measuring between 2.5 and 10 micrometers. Although larger than fine particles (PM2.5), PM10 particles are small enough to be drawn into the upper respiratory tract, nasal passages, and bronchi during outdoor breathing.

## Urban Origins and Mechanics
Unlike PM2.5, which is predominantly a product of fuel combustion, PM10 is heavily driven by mechanical abrasion and surface disturbance:
- **Road Dust Resuspension:** Heavy vehicular movement repeatedly kicks up settled mineral dust, dirt, and dried silt from roadways, especially during dry seasons.
- **Construction and Earthmoving:** Civil infrastructure development, unpaved shoulders, and roadside excavations.
- **Vehicle Wear Components:** Brake lining abrasion, clutch lining disintegration, and pavement wear.
- **Natural Environmental Sources:** Windblown desert dust, marine salt aerosols, and biological pollen.

## Dispersion and Spatial Patterns along Roads
PM10 concentrations exhibit pronounced localized peaks immediately adjacent to heavily trafficked roadways and active construction zones. Because coarse particles are heavier than fine particles, they settle out of the atmosphere more rapidly through gravitational settling, resulting in sharp concentration gradients within 50 to 100 meters of major roads.

## How CleanRoute AI Uses PM10 Data
CleanRoute AI models route-level PM10 to distinguish between heavy commercial transit corridors with high road dust and parallel residential or secondary roads with paved surfaces and lower mechanical disturbance. These metrics inform the composite air quality evaluation, providing an informational basis for choosing cleaner urban travel corridors.
