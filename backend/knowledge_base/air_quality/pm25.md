# Fine Particulate Matter (PM2.5)

## What is PM2.5?
PM2.5 designates microscopic airborne particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter of 2.5 micrometers or smaller—approximately 30 times smaller than the average human hair. Because of their minute size and light mass, PM2.5 particles remain suspended in the atmosphere for extended periods and travel considerable distances with wind currents.

## Primary Sources in Urban Environments
In urban and suburban transport corridors, PM2.5 originates primarily from:
- **Vehicular Combustion:** Direct exhaust emissions from diesel freight engines, older two-stroke vehicles, and gasoline engines.
- **Secondary Atmospheric Formation:** Chemical reactions between atmospheric precursor gases such as sulfur dioxide ($SO_2$), nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$), and volatile organic compounds ($VOCs$).
- **Friction and Abrasion:** Tire degradation, brake pad friction, and mechanical wear along congested highways.
- **Stationary Combustion:** Power generation facilities, industrial manufacturing, and biomass combustion.

## Inhalation Dynamics and Active Mobility
During physical exertion—such as brisk walking or active cycling—human minute ventilation (the volume of gas inhaled per minute) increases significantly (often 2 to 4 times resting volume). Cyclists and pedestrians traveling adjacent to heavy traffic corridors encounter elevated particulate concentrations directly at vehicle tailpipe height. 

Because PM2.5 particles are small enough to evade upper respiratory tract filtration and reach deep pulmonary alveoli, choosing alternative corridors located away from high-density freight corridors or buffered by vegetative barriers can contribute to lower estimated particulate inhalation.

## CleanRoute AI Exposure Estimation
CleanRoute AI continuously evaluates near-current hourly PM2.5 levels along sampled route coordinates. By combining average PM2.5 concentrations with travel duration, the system provides a normalized pollution exposure indicator to help users identify greener, lower-particulate travel paths. This information serves as informational guidance for route comparison and is not a medical diagnosis.
