# Urban Outdoor Pollution Dynamics

## Factors Governing Urban Air Dispersion
Outdoor air pollution is neither static nor uniformly distributed across an urban landscape. It behaves as a dynamic atmospheric fluid influenced by several interrelated physical forces:
- **Traffic Volume & Fleet Composition:** Peak concentrations correlate strongly with commuter rush hours and the density of diesel-powered heavy goods vehicles.
- **Wind Speed and Direction:** Moderate to strong winds accelerate pollutant dispersion and ventilation, diluting localized plumes. Calm, stagnant winds allow emissions to accumulate in local micro-basins.
- **Thermal Inversions:** When a layer of warm air sits atop a cooler ground layer, atmospheric convection is halted, trapping vehicle exhaust and particulate matter close to street level.
- **Micro-Climate and Vegetative Buffers:** Dense tree canopies, parklands, and vegetative barrier hedges act as physical barriers and settling substrates, lowering localized particulate concentrations by 15% to 35% compared to open roadway surfaces.

## Diurnal Concentration Curves
- **Morning Peak (07:00 - 10:00):** Sharp rise in $NO_2$ and $PM_{2.5}$ driven by heavy stop-and-go commuter traffic under low morning boundary layer conditions.
- **Midday Dispersion (11:00 - 14:00):** Solar heating drives atmospheric mixing and raises the planetary boundary layer, typically diluting primary particulate concentrations while initiating ozone formation.
- **Evening Peak (17:00 - 20:00):** Resurgence of vehicular exhaust emissions combined with cooling surface temperatures that compress the atmospheric mixing layer.

## How CleanRoute AI Leverages Dispersion Dynamics

This dynamic modeling ensures that route evaluations reflect current atmospheric conditions rather than static historical averages.
